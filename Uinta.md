# The Uinta Mountains and the evolving Colorado River system

This project explores how processes within the lithosphere and mantle interact with surface processes to shape the landscape of the American West. It brings together topographic analysis, landscape evolution and geological evidence to investigate active surface uplift and the history of river-network integration.

## Lithospheric processes and surface uplift

A central question is how deep-Earth processes can produce observable patterns of uplift at the surface. This work investigates the role of a lithospheric drip beneath the Uinta Mountains and what the resulting landscape can tell us about the timing and mechanisms of uplift.

## River integration and landscape evolution

The Green and Colorado rivers provide another window into the evolving landscape of the region. Research on their integration examines how changes in topography and underlying lithospheric structure can influence drainage development.

A related paper, *A lithospheric drip triggered Green and Colorado River integration*, is available in the [Journal of Geophysical Research: Earth Surface](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JF008733).
