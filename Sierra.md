# Surface uplift in the Sierra Nevada

Recent surface uplift of the Sierra Nevada has been the subject of considerable debate. The pattern and timing of uplift provide important clues about the processes operating beneath the range, including the possible influence of buoyant asthenosphere associated with the Isabella anomaly.

## Recovering uplift from river networks

We applied an inversion approach based on Fox (2019) to infer the pattern of surface uplift across the southern Sierra Nevada. By using the entire river network, rather than relying on isolated measurements, the method allows surface-uplift parameters to be resolved across the landscape.

## Key findings

The recovered pattern shows increased surface uplift in the centre of the range. This pattern is consistent with an interpretation involving support from buoyant asthenosphere associated with the Isabella anomaly, although the geological interpretation depends on the wider evidence used to constrain uplift.

The work demonstrates how river-network inversions can provide spatially resolved estimates of surface uplift and help test hypotheses about the processes shaping mountain ranges.
