# Rock uplift in the Wasatch Range

The Wasatch Range, Utah, lies on the eastern boundary of the Basin and Range Province, where deformation is accommodated by the Wasatch Fault. The fault is seismically active and sits beside Utah's most populated metropolitan areas, making its long-term behaviour important both for understanding regional tectonics and for assessing landscape response to fault activity.

Although fault slip has been characterised over timescales of tens of millions of years and over the last thousand years, evidence is limited for the timescales in between.

## Reconstructing a million years of uplift

River-network inversions can bridge this gap by recovering rock-uplift histories over million-year timescales. We used an inversion of three river networks draining the Wasatch Range, combining the results with field observations, apatite helium (AHe) thermochronology and erosion rates derived from cosmogenic nuclides to constrain the model.

## Key findings

The reconstructed history indicates that rock-uplift rates have increased and decreased over the last million years. Periods of enhanced uplift coincide with the development of pluvial lakes in the adjacent Bonneville basin. This relationship implicates lake-level changes in modulating fault slip and provides evidence that the mechanism described by Hetzel and Hampel (2005) operated through the Pleistocene.
